# Revenue options for CarworX

Author: Codex. Research date: 2026-10-27. Requested by owner during planning round 02. **Options and pricing hypotheses, not approved charges, advertiser commitments or revenue forecasts.** The free-validation decision D-002 remains in force. Sources substantiate existing mechanisms and constraints; prioritisation and proposed prices below are CarworX hypotheses.

## Recommendation

Keep basic customer discovery and a useful provider listing free. Validate demand and completed jobs first. After a separately approved pilot, test a core transaction/acquisition fee, optional provider software subscriptions, and a small amount of clearly labelled sponsorship. Add partner referrals selectively. Treat programmatic ads as supplementary income after useful content and traffic exist, rather than the foundation of the business.

Do not launch every revenue mechanism together or build an ad marketplace/credit wallet now. Measure whether each stream improves **net contribution**, provider retention and customer task completion. A paid provider must still pass the same relevance, service-area, availability and verification rules.

## Candidate streams

| Stream | Who pays / what they receive | Fit and timing | Main tradeoff / activation requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Booking commission | Provider pays a percentage or fixed platform fee on a completed, attributable job. | Strong core candidate after free pilot; recurring washes and standard jobs are easy to attribute. | Large repair invoices have parts/labour margin differences. Test category caps or labour-only basis where verifiable. Refund reversal and attribution rules required; platform revenue is not total booking value. |
| First-customer acquisition fee | Provider pays once for a genuinely new customer sourced by CarworX, with a lower/no acquisition fee on repeats. | Alternative to charging commission on every repeat booking; useful for recruitment. | Define new customer, attribution window and duplicate/returning-customer dispute rules. Do not impose both acquisition fee and commission by surprise. |
| Provider Pro subscription | Provider pays monthly for staff calendars, quote templates, reminders, reports, multi-branch tools or CRM. | Attractive recurring income once these tools demonstrably save time. Basic listing remains free. | Build paid tools in response to demand; churn/support costs matter. A subscription buys software, not a trust badge or immunity from poor reviews. |
| Sponsored provider placements | Provider buys an eligible municipality/category slot or time-limited promotion. | Early commercial experiment once local traffic is measurable. | Label "Sponsored"; preserve organic ranking and eligibility; do not sell false exclusivity or guaranteed jobs. Cap supply of slots and report delivered value. |
| Direct brand advertising | Car-care brands, tyre distributors, parts retailers or relevant national chains sponsor banners or a guide/section. | Can be sold manually with modest traffic if audience is relevant; demand is unverified. | Requires sales effort, creative review and a written delivery/refund agreement. No insurer/finance product advice implied. |
| Programmatic display ads | An approved network, e.g. Google AdSense, pays for eligible served inventory. | Later supplementary income on genuine public editorial content; earnings depend on traffic and auction outcomes. | Approval, content moderation, privacy/consent, ad blocking, fill and performance constraints. No guaranteed SA RPM or launch income. |
| Qualified lead fees | Provider pays for an opted-in, valid customer enquiry within its area/category. | Useful alternative for complex repairs that do not convert through checkout. | Define validity, duplicates, unreachable customers, delivery, credit/refund rules and how many providers receive a lead. Never sell customer details without an appropriate lawful basis and clear disclosure. |
| Ordinary commerce affiliate referrals | Retailer pays for attributable purchases of car-care supplies/accessories; licensed admin partner pays for eligible completed referrals. | Low fulfilment burden if suitable signed programmes exist. | Identify commercial links, returns/clawbacks and attribution; no partner access or rates assumed. Official fines links remain useful even without commission. |
| Fleet / employer accounts | Businesses pay per vehicle, user or month for consolidated booking, approvals, servicing records and reporting. | Later high-value B2B stream for small fleets, employer benefits, rental/delivery businesses. | Adds tenant permissions, invoice/reconciliation and support needs. Do not offer credit terms, warranty cover or emergency SLAs implicitly. |
| Customer membership | Driver pays for real negotiated discounts, family vehicles, reminders, concierge or an ad-free experience. | Later, only when tangible benefits exceed the fee. | Core fines referrals/search must remain accessible. Discount funding and priority capacity must be agreed with providers; no invented savings. |
| White-label / booking tools | Chains pay a setup/licence/support fee for a branded booking widget or multi-branch portal. | Later B2B extension of proven scheduling, not a second initial product. | Contractual support, integration, branding and data responsibilities; avoid uncontrolled bespoke forks. |
| Sponsored content and opted-in newsletter | Brand funds a useful clearly labelled article, comparison, seasonal campaign or newsletter placement. | Later editorial/retention channel with a real audience. | Editorial independence, substantiated claims, marketing consent and unsubscribe; ads never enter transactional/auth messages. |
| Concierge/admin service fee | Customer/business pays a disclosed fee for an actual coordination task or authorised renewal service. | Optional later convenience product. | Capacity, professional scope, cancellation and partner authority must be defined. No guaranteed fine reduction, legal representation or government affiliation. |
| Provider add-ons and integrations | Provider pays for additional messaging usage, calendar integrations, onboarding assistance or report exports. | Later optional Pro add-ons, with pass-through costs disclosed. | Don't charge for safety verification or basic corrections. Subscription economics must include vendor messaging and support costs. |
| Aggregated market reporting | Businesses pay for sufficiently aggregated, non-identifying demand benchmarks. | Distant option; no data-sales feature in v1. | Real anonymisation, minimum cohort sizes and rights review required. Exclude personal/vehicle/address/fines data and confidential competitor transactions. This is not permission to sell customer data. |

Insurance, extended warranties, repair finance and roadside subscriptions may have referral potential but are **separately gated**: identify regulated activity, authorised partners, remuneration/disclosure rules and required permissions before offering them. An affiliate label does not establish a regulatory exemption. Do not build insurance comparison, lending advice or cover in the initial marketplace.

## Website advertising proposal

### A. Sell direct contextual placements first

Start with a manually administered, fixed-term sponsorship rather than an auction. Suitable examples: a car-care brand sponsoring a maintenance guide; a relevant verified provider sponsoring a category/municipality result; an accessories retailer sponsoring an editorial section. Contacting advertisers and activating campaigns require later owner authorisation.

Proposed limits for the first experiment:

- One clearly labelled sponsored provider card per results page, visually distinct from organic results. It must satisfy the selected service/area filters and remain subject to suspension. Never show an out-of-area provider just because it paid.
- At most one direct banner on an eligible public page, below the main service choices/content; no pop-ups, autoplay, interstitials or sticky mobile overlays.
- No ad on authentication, identity verification, quote approval, checkout/payment, booking management, refunds/disputes, customer/provider/admin dashboards or fines-lookup/result screens. Keep fines referral guidance free of confusing commercial payment buttons.
- No paid inclusion in organic ratings, verification badges or default recommendations. A sponsor cannot suppress a negative review or buy an official endorsement.
- No competitor advertising on an individual provider's detail page in the initial experiment. Use category pages and useful guides instead.
- Reserve layout space, optimise creative size, review destinations, use safe external links, enforce dates/eligibility, and support immediate campaign suspension. Measure performance/task completion before expansion.

### B. Consider programmatic ads after content/traffic validation

Google [eligibility](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/9724?hl=en), [programme policies](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/48182?hl=en), [UGC responsibilities](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/1355699?hl=en) and [inventory balance policy](https://support.google.com/publisherpolicies/answer/11169917?hl=en) apply. An application, thin location page or empty results screen is not assumed to qualify.

Initially restrict any approved test to selected substantial public car-care articles. Do not enable sitewide automatic placements. Apply privacy/consent requirements for the actual audience and network before loading tags; contextual ads can still involve personal-data processing. Never transmit private account, ID, vehicle, booking, precise-location or fines information to an advertiser. Decide whether non-essential ad tags require consent in the actual deployment; a blanket POPIA "cookie exemption" is not assumed.

Moderate reviews/content on every monetised page. No clicking our own ads, incentivised clicks, fabricated traffic or tests against live ads. Approval and publisher revenue share are not an RPM guarantee. Use publisher-net page RPM for modelling; do not subtract the network share a second time.

### C. Measure value without invasive tracking

For direct campaigns prefer aggregate first-party delivery counts and optional disclosed campaign links; no cross-site retargeting in the first experiment. Define an impression metric before selling CPM inventory—server-render counts alone do not prove human viewability. Exclude bots/internal tests where feasible, and do not promise audited viewability.

Initial fixed-fee sponsorship reports can state dates/placement plus explicitly estimated first-party impressions/clicks. Distinguish delivered placement, clicks, qualified leads and completed jobs; never imply that clicks guarantee customers. No third-party script on sensitive workflows, even if a campaign would earn more there.

## Pricing hypotheses and worked arithmetic

**These examples are hypothetical inputs for discussion, not market rates, a forecast, a quote, or permission to charge.** Gross examples exclude applicable tax, payment processing, discounts, refunds, disputes, sales/support costs and other costs. Different models can charge for the same underlying demand: do not add all rows together as a forecast.

| Independent monthly example | Formula | Illustrative gross result |
|---|---|---|
| Provider Pro | 30 paying providers × R199/month | R5,970 |
| Completed-job commission | 500 jobs × R500 average eligible value × 5% | R12,500 from R250,000 eligible job value |
| Direct sponsored slots | 8 sold slots × R750/month | R6,000 |
| Qualified lead fees | 200 accepted, billable leads × R40 | R8,000 before disputed/credited leads |
| Programmatic ads: low illustrative RPM | 100,000 eligible pageviews ÷ 1,000 × R10 publisher-net page RPM | R1,000 |
| Programmatic ads: middle illustrative RPM | Same eligible views × R30/1,000 | R3,000 |
| Programmatic ads: high illustrative RPM | Same eligible views × R60/1,000 | R6,000 |

The RPM range is a sensitivity illustration, not researched South African pricing. The whole app's pageviews are not ad-eligible pageviews. Equivalent impression-CPM modelling must separately account for eligible slots, fill, viewability and net payout; do not mix page RPM and impression CPM.

Opportunity-cost test: if a completed job contributes R25 after variable costs, losing 120 jobs costs R3,000. An ad experiment earning R3,000 would not help if it caused that loss, before considering ad operations cost. Neither the R25 nor the conversion loss is measured today.

Track booked gross value, completed net-of-refund job value, platform fees billed, fees reversed, collected platform revenue and contribution separately. Do not report provider pass-through funds as CarworX revenue. Obtain accounting advice on principal/agent presentation and VAT obligations; do not assume every gross collection is a sale by CarworX.

## Staged experiments and exit decisions

1. **Free pilot:** instrument consent-aware funnel, demand, repeat use, provider response/completion and support/refund cost. Interview providers about value and willingness to pay. No current pilot fee decision is reversed.
2. **First revenue tests, after owner approval:** test an optional Pro package and/or a small commission/acquisition-fee cohort; separately test manual sponsorship with truthful audience figures. Do not silently stack fees. Define payer, price, fee basis, refunds, renewal/cancellation and success/stop criteria before each experiment.
3. **Validated expansion:** add vetted ordinary affiliate relationships or lead pricing for enquiries outside paid booking; sign attribution/data/refund terms. Consider fleet accounts after real business demand.
4. **Ads/content:** network approval and a controlled editorial-page test only after useful content, audience and privacy/performance readiness. Compare net ad income against conversion loss, Core Web Vitals and support complaints; disable an experiment that harms net contribution or task completion.
5. **Later products:** memberships, white-label, concierge, regulated referrals or benchmarking each require their own business case and approval.

For implementation planning, reserve room in accepted quote/payment snapshots for an explicit platform-fee basis and version. Do **not** build subscriptions, ad-serving, affiliate attribution, lead credits or reporting products before their experiment is approved. The first sponsor pilot can use approved static creative and manual invoicing; no wallet or bespoke billing engine.

## Evidence and limits

- [Snupit pricing](https://www.snupit.co.za/pricing.aspx) demonstrates a South African paid-lead model: credits used to respond to requests. This is evidence the mechanism exists, not evidence its rates will work for automotive providers.
- [Fresha pricing](https://www.fresha.com/pricing) demonstrates combining software subscriptions and marketplace acquisition fees. Its page is localised; no displayed foreign-currency price is treated as a South African benchmark.
- [AdSense revenue share](https://support.google.com/adsense/answer/180195?hl=en) explains the publisher share and eCPM bidding; it does not predict CarworX earnings.
- [ARB Code](https://www.arb.org.za/The_Codes/) and [general principles](https://arb.org.za/code-file-downloads/Section_II_General_Principles-v2026-04-07.pdf) inform identification and substantiation of ads. [Information Regulator guidance](https://inforegulator.org.za/guidance-notes/) informs direct marketing and data handling. Review the actual campaign and applicable rules before activation.
- [FSCA services](https://www.fsca.co.za/EServices/) identify FAIS licensing processes. The attempted intermediary-guidance PDF read timed out; no legal conclusion about exempt referrals is drawn from it. Regulated referrals remain unverified and deferred.

No advertiser, affiliate programme or provider has committed; no ad network application or account access has been verified. Traffic, margins, advertiser demand, achievable rates, conversion impact and willingness to pay are unmeasured. No sales message, billing integration, tracking tag or application code was created.
